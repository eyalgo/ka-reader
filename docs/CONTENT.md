## Voice

KA-Reader is talking to someone who has just pointed it at a folder they must not disturb —
often one an automated tool or a coding agent is changing while they watch (`docs/PRD.md`
§ Target Users). It speaks as itself: it names what it is looking at, says what it found, and
stops. Most of what is on the screen belongs to the reader — their files, their branch names,
their code, their Markdown — and KA-Reader's own words are the thin frame around them, so every
sentence it adds is taking space from the folder and has to be worth it. It never bids for
attention, never states something it has not read, and never reassures the reader that nothing
has been changed: the guarantee is in what the tool cannot do, not in what it says about itself.
When no rule below settles a sentence, write the one that says what KA-Reader observed, then what
you can do next, and end it there.

**These rules bind the words KA-Reader writes, not the words it shows.** A viewer's screen is mostly
somebody else's text: file contents, file and branch names, commit messages, rendered Markdown.
None of it is KA-Reader's writing, none of it is corrected, shortened, or re-punctuated, and no rule
in this document is applied to it. The rules govern the frame: labels, status messages, failure
text, the README, any text a release carries, and the repository's root documents written for a reader
outside the project, which are `SECURITY.md` and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`. The test is the reader and not
the directory: the reader of either is a stranger who reports a vulnerability or checks what KA-Reader is
built from, not a contributor or a build log. A root document not named here waits until one is cited.

The text the project's own checks print, the scripts under `checks/` and their suites, is outside these rules: it is not in the app
bundle, the app never shows it, and its reader is a contributor or a build log, not the person KA-Reader was pointed at a folder for.
Text that reaches the bundle or the app's screen is in scope whichever file it came from.

A licence's name and the text of a licence are the licence's words, carried unchanged, and no rule in this
document is applied to them; a sentence KA-Reader writes around one is its own writing and stays under every
rule. A name KA-Reader composes for a licence keeps the term the licence uses for its own grant, so
"GPL-2.0 with the linking exception" stands, while *exception* as a failure word in a sentence KA-Reader
writes stays banned. A name KA-Reader coins for a licence that gives itself none is its own writing and is
under the banned table.

**Who "you" is:** the person reading the folder. KA-Reader is the subject of a sentence about
what the tool did; *you* is the subject of a sentence about what the reader does. There is no
*we*: KA-Reader is a side project published under the maintainer's personal handle, and the tool
speaks as itself, never as a team or company (`docs/PRD.md` § Assumptions, **Public identity**).

**Conversational or casual:** KA-Reader is conversational — contractions, ordinary words, the way a
competent person would say it to another — and **not casual**. No jokes, no slang, no informal
interjections. The reason is the moment the words land in: the reader is mid-task, watching a
tree they must not disturb, and the sentences they read most are the ones that appear when
something failed. A joke arrives at the exact moment they are deciding whether this tool can be
trusted with a folder, and the product's character is "calm, truthful, still" (`docs/PRD.md`
§ UX Notes "The character").

## Tone

The voice does not change. What changes is how much of it is spent: how many sentences the moment
gets, and how much of the reader's attention KA-Reader is entitled to take.

**Across the first run — finding KA-Reader, starting it, and reading a first file** (`docs/PRD.md`
§ Success Metrics "Time-to-first-success"; `docs/UXD.md` UX-F1):

| Moment | Tone, and how far | Why it changes here |
|---|---|---|
| The README's first screen | The most words this product ever spends in one place, and every one of them a claim that can be checked in the next minute: what KA-Reader does, one way to install it, one thing to do first, and what it never touches. | It is the only surface that has to answer a stranger who has not started the tool, cannot ask anyone anything, and arrives expecting the documentation to be out of date. |
| Starting it | Silence, unless something failed, or the Open panel was dismissed before a folder was chosen — then one sentence naming the action that ends the wait (§ Message patterns, *Empty*). No banner, no version line, no greeting. | The reader started the tool in order to look at a folder; anything said before the folder appears is the tool talking about itself, except the one sentence that tells a reader with no folder yet how to choose one (`docs/UXD.md` UX-S4). |
| Startup fails | Three sentences at most: what KA-Reader was doing, what it found, what to do next. Written for someone who works with Git but is not necessarily an expert in it (`docs/PRD.md` § UX Notes "Failure states"). | This is the first minute, and it is the one moment where the reader has been stopped by the tool rather than by their folder. A dead end here ends the adoption. |
| Arrival, nothing open | One sentence, an instruction, no welcome. *"Select a file to view."* | There is nothing to set up, sign into, or dismiss (`docs/UXD.md` § 4), so the only thing to say is the one action available. |
| The first file opens | Nothing. The path line and the content are the whole message. | The product's job just happened; a message saying so would be KA-Reader congratulating itself. |
| A file that cannot be shown as text | One sentence, stating the fact about the file, with the tree still there as the way on. | It is not a failure of the reader or of the tool, and the reader's next move is simply another file. |

## Grammar and mechanics

- **Person.** *KA-Reader* is the subject of a sentence about what the tool did or found. *You* is the
  subject of a sentence about what the reader does. Never *we*, never *the user*, and never a
  passive that hides which of the two acted.
- **Tense.** Present for what is true now; simple past for what KA-Reader observed. No future tense
  about the tool's own behaviour except where a named condition follows: *"the reading panel shows the
  file as soon as KA-Reader can read it"*, never *"will refresh shortly"*.
- **Sentence case everywhere**, including labels, headings and controls. The only capitalised
  words inside a sentence are *KA-Reader*, *Git*, *Markdown*, *HEAD* — Git's own name, written as Git
  writes it (§ Message patterns, *The short status*) — and names taken from the reader's own
  folder. The Git view's section headings are written *Changes*, *Staged*, *Unstaged*,
  *Branches* and appear in capitals because the design language sets that level in uppercase
  (`docs/DESIGN.md` § Typography, `{typography.label}`); the string is written in sentence case
  and the transform is visual, so nothing in the source is typed in capitals.
- **Full stops.** A full sentence ends with a full stop, including a one-sentence status message.
  A label or a control's text carries none: *Files*, *Git*, *Light*, *Dark*, *Rendered*,
  *Source*, *Browse…*, *Quit*.
- **Ellipsis.** The character `…` appears in exactly one place: at the end of a control that opens
  something asking the reader for more — *Browse…* (`docs/DESIGN.md` § Components, "The menu"). It
  never marks work in progress, and no message trails off.
- **Exclamation marks and question marks.** None, anywhere. KA-Reader asks the reader nothing (there
  is no confirmation, no prompt, no dialogue — `docs/UXD.md` UX-I7), and it raises its voice
  nowhere.
- **Contractions** are used — *isn't*, *can't*, *doesn't* — everywhere except inside a standing
  sentence (§ Terminology, *Standing sentences*), whose wording is fixed as written.
- **Quotation marks and emphasis.** No bold and no italics in any string the running product
  shows; the design language has no emphasised text token, and emphasis inside a status message
  is an attention bid. A name taken from the folder — a path, a branch, a file — is never put in
  quotation marks; the type it is drawn in is the design language's (`docs/DESIGN.md` § Typography).
- **Joining a name to a word in a label.** Where a label puts a name taken from the folder before a
  word KA-Reader writes, the name is the row's own text, unchanged, then a space, U+2014 and a space,
  then the word, and the label carries no full stop: *"README.md — modified"*. The word is always
  last, so a name that itself holds a spaced U+2014 still reads right. This is the one dash KA-Reader's
  own writing uses. A hyphen or an en dash does not read as a join, because names in the folder
  already carry hyphens; a colon reads as tool output after a path; a bare space or parentheses do
  not separate a name from a word at a glance; and a verb is ruled out because *conflict* is a noun
  where the other five words are participles. This is stated for this shape only: a label that puts
  the word first, carries two words or adds a count is not covered until one occurs.
- **Numbers.** Numerals always, including one to nine: every number this product shows is counted
  from the folder — a count beside a heading, a line number, a commit's position. No number is
  spelled out, and no message rounds one.
- **Dates and times.** Absolute, as `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`, in the reader's own local time. Never
  relative (*"2 days ago"*): the reader is comparing commits with each other, and a relative time
  changes between two glances at the same screen while the commit does not.
- **Duration.** No message states how long anything will take. Where the reader has to wait, the
  message names the condition that ends the wait, not a number — the product's own refresh
  threshold is undecided (`docs/PRD.md` § Success Metrics "Refresh immediacy", *"exact latency
  threshold TBD"*), so a duration in a string would be a number nobody has measured.
- **Units and currency.** Neither appears. No surface this product has shows a file size, a
  transfer rate, or a price. If a state ever needs a size, it is written here first.
- **Abbreviations.** None are coined. *Git* is capitalised as the name of the system and lowercase
  only inside a literal command (`git init`). *Markdown* is capitalised. *KA-Reader* is capitalised
  in prose; the lowercase `ka-reader` appears only as a repository or package identifier, which is a
  name and not a word. The product's name is never written in kanji, and never appears in the
  running product (`docs/DESIGN.md` § Components: the shell carries no product name at all).
- **A single-letter code is never the whole of a statement.** Git's status letters are shown
  beside a changed file or a listed folder (`docs/DESIGN.md` § Components, "Status letters");
  every place they appear is covered by § Accessibility of text, which states what has to name
  them in words.

### Standing sentences

A standing sentence is a promise or a stated limit that appears on more than one surface. Each has
**one wording**, given here, and every surface that states it carries that wording — no paraphrase,
no shortened version, and no softening with *generally*, *usually* or *by default*. These are rules
about wording, not copy for a screen: their whole point is that the words do not vary.

| What it states | The wording |
|---|---|
| Read-only — the product's defining promise (`docs/PRD.md` § Success Metrics "Read-only integrity") | **"KA-Reader never changes the folder you point it at. It writes no file in it, and it runs no Git command that alters your repository's state."** |
| The network (`docs/PRD.md` § Data & Privacy; `docs/SRS.md` NFR-8, *"none, not merely no telemetry"*) | **"KA-Reader sends no telemetry, and it makes no network call while it runs — downloading it uses the network; using it does not."** |
| Before 1.0.0 (`docs/PRD.md` § Assumptions, "Versioning") | **"KA-Reader is before version 1.0.0. Until 1.0.0, anything in it may change: what it does, how it looks, and how you start it."** Retired the day 1.0.0 is released; what replaces it is written then. |
| The beta's Git view (`docs/PRD.md` § Scope (In), "The first public release — a beta") | **"The Git view and the letters beside changed files show the Git state KA-Reader read when you opened the folder. They do not update while KA-Reader is open."** Retired the day the Git view follows the disk (`docs/SDD.md` § 6 S-8); nothing is written in its place. The row fixes this statement and no other. The README's known gaps also say that a repository created at the folder's root after the folder is opened is not followed, but that sentence appears on the README alone, so it is not a standing sentence and has no row; it is written with the README, to this document's rules. No other sentence on the Git view restates the limit: the short status and the *Empty* sentence stay in the present tense. |
| The support posture (`docs/PRD.md` § Assumptions, "Support posture") | **"Issues and questions are welcome and are read when there is time. | Archiving (`docs/PRD.md` § Assumptions) | **"If KA-Reader can no longer be maintained, this repository is archived on that day, so you can see it rather than guess."** |

Whether the product states a promise at all is the PRD's, and whether a promise is true of the
built system is the SRS's. This table fixes only the words, once the promise exists.

## Message patterns

Five standing states. Every message KA-Reader writes is an instance of one of them, except the one
sentence that reports Git's own state rather than KA-Reader's work (*The short status*, below); and
*Error* has two classes that are chosen between before a word is written.

**One surface carries no words at all, and that is a decision made here rather than a gap.** In a
folder KA-Reader has found is not the root of a Git repository, the Git view is present and shows
nothing: no message, no label, no explanation (`docs/UXD.md` UX-I17). It is a working state and not
a failure, so no pattern below reaches it and none is written for it. The reason is that there is
nothing truthful to say: the two next steps an editor offers on this same surface — create a
repository, publish it — are writes KA-Reader can never make (`docs/trail/prd/cycle-10.md` § 2.2), and
with no action to name, a sentence here could only explain the view's own emptiness, which is the
tool talking about itself. The cost was accepted where the decision was made: an empty view cannot
by itself tell this folder apart from a repository KA-Reader failed to read, and the failure the reader
must know about is written as an unexplained fault in this same slot, never as a message in this
state.

### Error

**Two classes, and which one you are writing is decided before the wording.** They differ on one
question: **is there something KA-Reader actually observed?** A file that is not there, a path that
held nothing, a lock file that exists — KA-Reader read the folder and can name what it found. An
unexplained fault — it stopped, and nothing it read accounts for it — has nothing to name, and the
first formula applied to it produces a sentence that says a thing went wrong and nothing else.
Both classes carry no apology, and neither ever passes through text from inside the tool
(§ Terminology, *Banned*).

**"No cause" in both formulas means no *mechanical* cause** — no error code, no library name, no
internal state — and never "don't say why". Where KA-Reader read the thing that explains the failure,
naming it *is* naming what it observed, and the first formula's second clause is where it goes.

#### Error — an observed condition

**Formula:** *What KA-Reader was doing* + *what it observed, naming the file, path or state* + *what
you can do next, or that there is nothing to do and what ends the wait.* One sentence for each
part, three sentences at most, in that order. No cause. No apology. The surface keeps a way on
(`docs/UXD.md` UX-I14), and the sentence names it where it is not already on screen.

**Examples:**
- *"KA-Reader looked for `/Users/you/code/app` and didn't find it. Check the path and start KA-Reader
  again."*
- *"KA-Reader couldn't read this repository's Git status when you opened the folder, and
  `.git/index.lock` was there. Start KA-Reader again to read it again."* — the Git view's lock **while
  Git state is read once, at open (the beta)**. The lock is stated as observed at open, in the simple
  past, and as a file seen, never as a cause. The way on is the formula's other branch, *what you can do
  next*. The sentence does not say a restart will succeed: the lock is commonest when an agent is
  running Git, and a restart can meet it again. Its first sentence says *when* because that is the
  failure's own time, and not the beta's limit restated (§ Terminology, *Standing sentences*).
- *"KA-Reader can't read this repository's Git status while `.git/index.lock` is there. There's
  nothing to do: the Git view shows the status as soon as KA-Reader can read it."* — the same view once
  Git state follows the disk, written for that day. It replaces the beta's sentence when the beta's
  statement is retired, and until then it is shown nowhere, because it would promise a read that does
  not come. The two are two whole sentences and never one with a switch inside (§ Localization).

**Where a message's way on is a later read of Git state, the beta's form takes the other branch.** In
the beta nothing reads Git state again until the folder is opened again, which in v1 means starting
KA-Reader again (`docs/UXD.md` UX-S4). A Git-view message that ends *"…as soon as KA-Reader can read
it"* would promise a read that does not come, so in the beta it states what KA-Reader observed when you
opened the folder and names starting KA-Reader again. This holds for every message in the Git view
whose way on is a later read, not for the lock message alone.

#### Error — an unexplained fault

**Formula:** *What KA-Reader was doing* + *that it stopped and cannot tell why* + *what is unaffected,
and the one thing you can do next.* Three sentences at most, in that order. The middle sentence is
the whole difference: KA-Reader states the limit of what it knows rather than naming a cause it never
read, because a cause guessed here is the first failed claim the reader can check.

**This class never widens beyond its own failure.** It is written in the failure slot of the item
that stopped — the open file, the tree row, the Git view, the startup surface — and names what still
works, because an unexplained fault in one file is not a statement about the folder
(`docs/UXD.md` UX-I16, which puts it in that item's own slot; `docs/SRS.md` NFR-3, a failing item
degrades only itself). And the route out is on the surface (`docs/UXD.md` UX-I14): an issue tracker
is not a route out and never stands alone, so no message in this class ends by asking the reader to
report it. UX-I16 states that limit too, and adds the one this document is the reason for: no
control anywhere reveals, expands or copies the fault's detail, so there is no such control to write
a label for and no exception owed to § Terminology, *Banned*.

**A stopped read is never dressed as a pending one.** Once KA-Reader has stopped, § Message patterns
*Loading* does not apply to it: a *"Reading this file."* left standing over a fault claims work that
is not happening, which is the one thing every message here may not do (`docs/UXD.md` UX-I16,
UX-I13).

**A permission that was refused is not this class** — a refusal KA-Reader read is something it
observed, and it takes the first formula: *"KA-Reader can't read this folder."* This class is for the
fault with nothing behind it the tool can name.

**Examples:**
- *"KA-Reader stopped while reading this file and can't tell why. The rest of the folder is still
  readable — select another file."*
- *"KA-Reader stopped while reading this repository's Git status and can't tell why. The file tree is
  unaffected. Start KA-Reader again to read the status again."* — while Git state is read once, at
  open (the beta). The tree stays the unaffected part and starting KA-Reader again is the one thing to
  do next.
- *"KA-Reader stopped while reading this repository's Git status and can't tell why. The file tree is
  unaffected; the Git view shows the status again if KA-Reader can read it."* — once Git state follows
  the disk, written for that day. It replaces the beta's sentence when the beta's statement is retired
  and is shown nowhere until then.

### Empty
**Formula:** *What is not there*, stated as a fact about the folder rather than about KA-Reader,
plus *the one action available* where there is one. One sentence. Never a blank surface, and never
shown before KA-Reader has read the state it is reporting (`docs/UXD.md` UX-I13).

**Examples:**
- *"Select a file to view."* — the reading panel with nothing open; the action is the whole
  message.
- *"Nothing has changed in this repository."* — the Git view on a clean repository, shown only
  after Git state has been read.
- *"Choose a folder to view."* — the startup surface when the Open panel was dismissed before any
  folder was chosen (`docs/UXD.md` UX-S4). Nothing failed, so there is no failure sentence; the
  surface's two controls, *Browse…* and *Quit*, are its routes, and the sentence names the one that
  ends the empty state.

### Loading
**Formula:** *Reading* + *the thing being read.* Present participle, one clause, ending in a full
stop. No percentage, no estimate, no ellipsis, and no claim of progress the tool has not measured.
Where the reader can do nothing but wait, the empty half of the surface stays empty rather than
showing a state that has not been read.

**Examples:**
- *"Reading Git status."*
- *"Reading this folder."* — a folder in the file tree that has not been read yet.

### Partial
**Formula:** *What is shown* + *what is not shown, marked in the place it would have been* +
*which of the two the reader is holding.* The missing piece is named where it belongs, never
summarised in a banner over the whole surface, because the rest of the surface is still true.

**Anything outside the folder is said one way.** A link, an image, or anything else that leads
outside the folder KA-Reader is showing is described as being *outside the folder you opened*, and
KA-Reader *doesn't read it*. Never *this folder* for the opened folder — on a tree row that phrase
already means the row's own folder. And never a wider claim such as *"KA-Reader reads nothing outside
it"*: KA-Reader reads its own remembered state from outside the folder (`docs/UXD.md` UX-I9), so the
wider sentence is false, which is the shape RN-16 corrected. *Doesn't*, not *can't*: this is
KA-Reader's rule, not something it is unable to do.

**Examples** — on a tree row, with the rest of the file tree standing (`docs/SRS.md` NFR-3;
`docs/fdr.md` R-33, R-34):
- *"KA-Reader can't read this folder."*
- *"This link leads outside the folder you opened, so KA-Reader doesn't read it."*
- *"This link loops back on itself, so KA-Reader can't read it."*
- *"This link leads to something that isn't there."* — an entry whose target was missing when its
  folder was listed.
- *"This link leads through more links than KA-Reader follows."* — the limit is KA-Reader's, so the
  sentence puts it on KA-Reader rather than calling the reader's links *too many*. It states no
  number, because that number is not counted from the folder (§ Grammar and mechanics, *Numbers*).
- *"This folder is no longer there."* — a folder row KA-Reader listed, and then found nothing at when
  it opened it. It names no cause: a folder moved, renamed or deleted looks the same to KA-Reader.

**Something that is not there is worded by what KA-Reader observed, not by what the row is.** Two
observations reach it, and each has its own sentence. When an entry's target was missing at the
moment its folder was listed, the entry takes the link's sentence, because only a link reaches
that condition at listing, and *no longer there* would claim the target was once there. When KA-Reader
listed a folder and later found nothing at it, the row takes *"This folder is no longer there."*,
because KA-Reader saw both halves: the folder listed, and nothing in its place. That holds for a
folder row reached through a link as well: whether the link went or its target did, the folder the
row showed is no longer there. Neither sentence is true of the other case, so they stay two whole
sentences and never become one sentence with a switch inside it (§ Localization).

None of these names a next step: the rest of the tree is the way on, and it is already on screen
(`docs/SDD.md` § 4 C-E4).

**Examples** — in rendered Markdown, in the box where an image would sit, which also holds the
image's own alt text and its source (`docs/UXD.md` UX-I12; `docs/DESIGN.md` § Components, "Rendered
Markdown"):
- *"This image is outside the folder you opened, so KA-Reader doesn't read it."* — a remote address
  and a path that escapes the folder alike.
- *"There's no file where this image points."* — an image inside the folder whose file is missing.
- *"KA-Reader can't read this image."* — an image inside the folder whose file is there but
  unreadable.
- *"KA-Reader doesn't show images in this format."* — a file inside the folder in a format KA-Reader
  does not draw. The limit is KA-Reader's, so the file is never called *unsupported*.

**Example** — at the end of the history, where the history shown stops short of the repository's
(`docs/UXD.md` UX-S6):
- *"Older commits aren't shown."* — no number, and nothing offers to load more. Where the whole
  history fits, nothing is said.

### Recovered
**Formula:** *Nothing.* The failure or pending sentence disappears and the state takes its place.
No message announces a recovery, and nothing is apologised for. A recovery message would be a
second interruption for something the reader can already see, and *"back to normal"* is a claim
about a cause KA-Reader never observed.

**Examples:**
- Git state becomes readable again: *"KA-Reader can't read this repository's Git status…"* is
  replaced by the branch, the changed files and the staged files. Nothing else appears.
  In the beta this case does not occur: nothing reads Git state again after the folder is opened, so
  the sentence stays until the folder is opened again (§ Error, *an observed condition*). The example is
  written for the day Git state follows the disk.
- A file that had vanished is written again: the panel's *"This file is no longer there."* is
  replaced by the file's content. No *"restored"*, no *"back"*.

### The short status — Git's state, in Git's word

The Git view's short status is the one sentence KA-Reader writes about Git's state rather than about
its own work, so none of the five formulas applies to it. It is shown only once Git state has been
read (`docs/UXD.md` UX-I13), and it says one of three things (`docs/UXD.md` UX-S3;
`docs/fdr.md` R-162 – R-165):

- **Clean** — the working tree and the index hold no change: the *Empty* sentence, *"Nothing has
  changed in this repository."*
- **Changed, in the normal state** — nothing. The changed and staged lists and their counts
  already say it, and the status adds no count.
- **Mid-operation, or HEAD detached** — one whole sentence naming the state by the word Git itself
  uses for it: *merge*, *rebase*, *cherry-pick*, *revert*, *bisect*, *am session*, or *HEAD* being
  *detached*. That is seven states and seven whole sentences, never one sentence with the operation
  substituted in (§ Localization). Where two hold at once, as when a rebase or an am session runs
  on a detached HEAD, the operation is the one named (`docs/fdr.md` R-165; RN-36).

It never names a remote, never gives a count, and never uses a word Git does not use for the
state — not *paused*, *stuck*, *interrupted* or *broken*, each of which judges a state the reader
may be in on purpose. It does not name the branch being rebased, a case the view leaves uncovered
(`docs/UXD.md` UX-S3).

**Examples:**
- *"A rebase is in progress in this repository."*
- *"An am session is in progress in this repository."*
- *"HEAD is detached in this repository."*

## Accessibility of text

This is KA-Reader's own bar for text, and it sits inside the posture the product has already
declared: v1 is mouse-driven, it makes no keyboard-accessibility promise, and beyond that it makes
no assistive-technology promise (`docs/UXD.md` UX-A1). Nothing here claims conformance with any
standard, and nothing here promises anything to a screen reader. What it does is make the
product's own words legible to everyone reading them on the screen.

- **A word carries every state distinction that a colour carries.** The visual half of this rule
  is settled (`docs/UXD.md` UX-A2); the verbal half is that the word exists and is on the surface —
  a heading, a letter with its expansion, or the sentence itself. A state whose only text is a
  colour name has no text.
- **A single letter is not a word, so the view expands it.** Git's status letters — `A`, `M`,
  `D`, `R`, `C`, `U` — are shown beside a changed file or a listed folder, and resting the pointer
  **on the letter** names the row — the file or the folder — and then the letter in words:
  *added*, *modified*, *deleted*, *renamed*, *untracked*, *conflict*. On a folder row the same six
  words apply as written and a folder takes no word of its own: a submodule whose commit moved
  is **M** and reads *modified*, and a repository nested in the folder you opened is **U** and
  reads *untracked*. The words speak of that listed folder alone, never of anything beneath it.
  **A**, **D**, **R** and **C** on a folder row are not stated until a site shows one. The expansion lives in the view, on demand — not as a permanent legend
  beside the rows, and not as a list in the README the reader has already left. What raises it and
  how it is drawn are the design language's (`docs/DESIGN.md` § Components, "The tooltip"); the two
  words it carries, and their order, are this document's.
  **Those six words are the letters' meanings in this product and are not Git's porcelain
  vocabulary.** `U` is *untracked* and `C` is *conflict*, which is what the editor this product
  resembles means by them and what `docs/DESIGN.md` § Components ("Status letters") draws; a
  porcelain reading — copied, unmerged — would tell the reader the wrong thing about their own
  repository. Where these letters are named anywhere else the product speaks, they are named in
  these same six words.
- **Alternative text describes the state, not the glyph.** The fourteen icons this product ships
  never stand alone (`docs/UXD.md` UX-A4), so no icon is given text describing its shape. The
  words beside it are the state; an icon whose meaning is not already in those words is an icon
  this product does not use.
- **Link text means something read on its own.** In KA-Reader's own writing — the README above all —
  no link is called *here*, *this*, *link*, or *read more*; the link's text names what is at the
  other end. Inside rendered Markdown the reader's own link text stands as written, and the
  destination is shown beside it (`docs/DESIGN.md` § Components, "Rendered Markdown"), so a
  reader is never asked to guess where a link goes.
- **A failure names the thing it is about, in the same sentence.** There is no form and no field
  in this product, so the tie between an error and its subject is made by naming the file, the
  path or the view inside the message, rather than by placing the message near something.
- **The reading bar, stated as a rule rather than a grade.** Every word in a message KA-Reader writes
  is one of four things: a word the README already uses, a name taken from the reader's own
  folder, a word Git itself uses for the state being reported — *branch*, *changed*,
  *staged*, *commit*, *untracked*, *conflict* — or a word § Terminology fixes. A word that is none
  of the four does not go in a message. The third source is wide because the reader is assumed to
  know Git and the product may leave Git's own vocabulary unexplained (`docs/PRD.md` § Goals, "No
  learning curve"); it is not unlimited, because a word Git does not use for the state in hand is
  this tool's own jargon however technical it sounds. The fourth is how a concept the README never
  needed to name — a *link* on a tree row, an *image* in rendered Markdown — reaches a message
  without becoming jargon: a word enters § Terminology only with its concept, the names it was
  chosen over, and the reason, which is the argument a word needs before a reader meets it. The bar
  stays checkable against one string and this document without knowing anything about the reader
  — which is the test, and the reason it is four sources rather than a grade.

## Localization

KA-Reader ships in English, and no translation is planned (`docs/PRD.md` § UX Notes, "UI language:
English"). The rules below hold anyway. They cost nothing while a string is being written and
cannot be applied afterwards, and the product is public: it is read by people whose first language
is not English whether or not it is ever translated.

- **One message is one whole sentence in the source.** No message is built at runtime by joining
  fragments, and no fragment is stored as a separate string to be assembled. A message with two
  cases is two whole sentences, not one sentence with a switch inside it.
- **Every placeholder is named**, never positional: `{path}`, `{branch}`, `{file}`, `{count}`. A
  placeholder holds a value taken from the reader's folder or machine — a path, a name, a
  count. It never holds a piece of KA-Reader's own prose, because a fragment substituted into a
  sentence cannot be read, translated or checked on its own.
- **No sentence carries grammar only English has.** No plural made by adding *s* to a word beside
  a count, and no article chosen from the next word's first letter. Where a count and a noun must
  appear together, they appear as the design language already puts them — a heading with a numeral
  beside it, *Changes 3* (`docs/DESIGN.md` § Components) — which is not a sentence and inflects in
  no language.
- **Every message carries its context in the source, beside the string:** what the reader was doing
  when it appears, and what each placeholder holds. That note is writable only at the moment the
  string is written, because that is the only moment anyone knows what the reader was doing. Its
  reader is not only a translator who may never arrive — it is the next person to change that
  string.
- **Never translated:** the product's name (*KA-Reader*), file
  paths, branch names, commit identifiers, Git's status letters, the licence text, and any
  environment variable a surface names. These are identifiers, not words.
- **A constraint the words impose on the layout:** a status message is prose on a surface, never
  text in a row that clips (`docs/DESIGN.md` § Components, "Status messages"). Rows clip and file
  names are the reader's, so clipping one costs nothing; clipping a sentence costs its second
  half, which is where every message here puts what the reader can do next.
